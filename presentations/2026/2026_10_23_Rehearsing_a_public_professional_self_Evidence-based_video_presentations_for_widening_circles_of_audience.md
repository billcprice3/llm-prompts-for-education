### The Background

* Learning is social and tool-mediated (Vygotsky, 1978). Newcomers enter a field through **legitimate peripheral participation**, moving from the margins of a community of practice toward fuller participation (Lave & Wenger, 1991).
* New professionals try on **"provisional selves"** and refine them through feedback (Ibarra, 1999). Recent work in career construction theory calls for career interventions suited to the digital age, including digital storytelling (Wang & Li, 2024).
* **Curated online work** serves process, assessment, and showcase purposes, each with a different audience, and their portability after graduation is a persistent challenge (Carter, 2021). A repository deposit gives students a stable, institutional link they can carry outward.
* Curated online work **signals professional identity** to potential employers (Anos, 2025). Large-scale experiments on LinkedIn found that moderately weak ties produced the most job mobility, especially in digital industries (Rajkumar et al., 2022).

---

### The Intervention: Evidence-based video presentations for (semi-) public consumption

* In place of in-person final presentations, students produce an evidence-based video presentation in either traditional "canned lecture" or modern vertical video format.
* Students deposit these video presentations on D-Scholarship accessible either to just the Pitt community or to the general public. Students are encouraged, but not required, to publish to social media as well.

---

### Motivating Question

> Can graduate students achieve **legitimate peripheral participation** and try on **provisional professional selves** through public-facing video presentations?

### Student Privacy & Autonomy

* Students in INFSCI 2205 are first-year international graduate students in the School of Computing and Information, many of whom face heightened scrutiny of their online presence, including expanded visa-related social media vetting and recent SEVIS terminations. They deserve to control their digital footprint.
* Video visibility is student-controlled: from a Pitt-only deposit, to a repository link on a CV or professional profile, to fully public sharing on social media platforms. Students receive credit for D-Scholarship deposit, but social media distribution is optional. Students rehearse the workflow of public professional or scholarly communication while deciding for themselves how far their work travels today.

---

### Activity Development and Status within INFSCI 2205

* **2010s:** Traditional in-class final presentations
* **Early 2020s:** Experimentation with remote and pre-recorded presentations; formalization of Canvas discussion board-based "digital colloquium" format
* **2025:** Increased emphasis on teaching video creation as professional identity formation; exploration of how young professionals authentically use modern vertical video
* **2026, forthcoming:** Introduction of the D-Scholarship video submission requirement and optional social media distribution
